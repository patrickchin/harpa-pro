title: "How to place your first small order from China"
description: "Plan a limited trial order from China with clear specifications, sample approval, payment stages, inspection, and a delivery review."

---

A first order should test a specific buying process. Choose a product you
can specify clearly, order a quantity you can afford to evaluate, and
define what must happen before you repeat it.

For a designer sourcing furniture, a trial might cover one chair model
for a small room rather than the whole building. For a contractor, it
might cover a limited batch of standard hardware. Keep customisation
manageable while you learn how the supplier communicates and delivers.

## Check the destination before choosing the quantity

Identify who will import the goods, how they will clear customs, and where
they will be received. Ask a broker about the exact product, destination,
documentation, and likely charges before committing to the order.

A small quantity does not establish an exemption from product rules or
import requirements. Have the broker assess the real shipment. Use the
country sections in the [main buying guide](/procurement/how-to-buy-from-china#1-check-port-clearance-and-import-requirements)
to start that conversation.

Include clearance and transport in the trial budget. Some charges may
apply to the shipment rather than each unit, so reducing the quantity
can raise the delivered unit cost. Ask for the actual charge basis.

## Set the purpose and limit of the trial

Write down what the order should establish. You might need to confirm a
finish, check assembly on site, test export packing, or assess the
supplier's ability to follow your approved drawing.

Agree a spending limit and an approval owner. State which results would
justify another order and which would require changes. If several people
are involved, name who approves the sample, pays the invoice, and accepts
delivery.

Set a useful receiving date and work back through sample approval,
production, inspection, dispatch, clearance, and final delivery. Ask each
party to confirm its own stage. Keep a separate allowance for corrections
instead of treating the first proposed dispatch date as the project
completion date.

## Ask for a quotation that matches the trial

Send shortlisted suppliers the same brief. Include dimensions, material,
finish, quantity, packaging, destination, and any necessary technical
documents. Ask them to identify assumptions and exclusions.

Request the minimum order for that exact specification. A supplier may
offer a smaller trial using a stock finish or standard packaging. Compare
that option with a customised batch, including any setup costs and its
usefulness for your project.

Confirm what starts the lead time: payment, drawing approval, sample
approval, or receipt of a component. Write the trigger and agreed
delivery term into the quotation. Keep the version you accept.

## Approve a sample and the written specification

Pay for a sample where that helps you assess the product. Ask whether it
is existing stock or made to your specification. Confirm the courier cost
and whether a later order receives a sample credit.

Inspect the sample against your requirements and record measurements and
photographs. Test assembly and interfaces where relevant. A cabinet hinge
may look right but need a different drilling pattern from the joinery
already designed.

Alibaba.com's buyer guidance includes samples and detailed purchase orders
among the steps used to prepare an order. Preserve the approved sample
reference and put the accepted requirements in the order documents.
[Alibaba.com: preparing and placing an order](https://activity.alibaba.com/page/how_it_wroks.html)

Keep a reference sample available to the person checking production.
Record any accepted differences in writing. A photographed sample and a
verbal approval leave too much room for misunderstanding.

## Agree payment and inspection stages together

Check the supplier's legal identity and independently verify payment
instructions. If a deposit is requested, agree its amount and the work
it authorises. Record when the remaining payments are due and what
evidence must be available at each stage.

Avoid assuming that one deposit percentage suits every product or order.
Discuss the actual materials, production work, payment method, and
acceptance conditions. Keep the agreed terms with the invoice.

If using platform protection, confirm that the exact order and payment
route are eligible. Save the online order and communicate material
specification changes through the supported process.

Book the quality check early enough to leave time for a correction.
For a short batch, discuss with the inspector whether full checking of
certain characteristics is practical. Agree the checks and decision
rules before production finishes.

## Inspect the goods before dispatch

Check quantity, product identity, workmanship, function, dimensions,
packaging, and shipping marks against the agreed scope. Some technical
requirements need separate testing by a competent laboratory.

SGS describes final random inspection as a check of finished goods using
an agreed sampling approach and a report. Confirm your provider's actual
scope and production-readiness requirements before booking.
[SGS: final random inspection](https://www.sgs.com/en/services/final-random-inspection)

Review the report before authorising dispatch or a payment tied to
acceptance. If something fails, document the correction, who pays for
it, and how the revised goods will be checked. Keep the shipping deadline
separate from the acceptance decision.

## Receive the order with its records ready

Before arrival, confirm the delivery address, unloading arrangement, and
receiving contact. Have the order, packing list, invoice, and transport
details available to the broker and receiving team as needed.

Count the packages and record their condition. Photograph visible damage
and retain packaging where a claim may be necessary. Check the contents
promptly and follow the applicable carrier, insurer, or platform process
within its stated deadlines.

## Decide what should change on the repeat order

Compare actual cost and timing with the budget. Record defects, missing
parts, packing problems, and time spent resolving questions. Ask the people
installing or using the product what they found.

Write a short decision: repeat unchanged, repeat with specified changes,
or stop. Update the specification and approved sample record before
requesting a larger quotation. A trial earns its value when the next
order uses what you learned.
