This is a hypothetical worked example. The buyer, products, quantities,
prices, and decisions are invented to explain the purchasing process.
They describe no customer transaction, current market quotation, or
promised outcome. Destination requirements must be checked for a real
shipment.

The example follows an interior designer buying 40 dining chairs for a
hospitality project. The chairs need to match a signed-off finish sample
and reach a receiving warehouse before installation begins.

## 1. Define the order and check the import route

The designer prepares a chair schedule with dimensions, frame material,
upholstery reference, colour, construction details, intended use, and
quantity. A drawing identifies the critical dimensions. Photographs show
the desired appearance but do not replace the specification.

Before requesting production, the buyer asks a destination customs
representative to confirm classification, import eligibility, documentary
requirements, and applicable charges. A qualified product specialist
checks which performance and compliance evidence the intended use requires.
Those answers become conditions of the purchase.

The buyer also confirms the receiving warehouse's access and unloading
arrangements. The project site has limited storage, so the order will be
received and checked elsewhere before installation.

## 2. Send the same enquiry to three suppliers

Each supplier receives the same specification revision and delivery
scope. The buyer asks for a unit price, minimum quantity, sample cost,
production assumptions, packaging details, and any exclusions.

One quotation omits the specified upholstery. Another includes it but
proposes a different frame section. The buyer asks for revised quotations
before comparing prices. The remaining differences are recorded openly.

Supplier checks examine the contracting entity, manufacturing location,
relevant production capability, and payment beneficiary. The designer
keeps the evidence and identifies which manufacturing steps use other
companies. A well-presented listing alone does not complete those checks.

## 3. Approve a sample with recorded references

The buyer orders a sample and checks it against the schedule. Its fabric
matches, but the seat height differs from the drawing. The supplier
produces a corrected sample before the production order is confirmed.

The designer records the approved sample reference, drawing revision,
finish reference, and agreed checks. Any necessary testing is completed
or reviewed for the final configuration. A retained sample gives the
inspection team a physical reference for appearance and workmanship.

Alibaba's sourcing guidance identifies detailed purchase orders, samples,
and inspections as steps buyers can use when purchasing through its
platform. The example uses those purchasing controls without assuming
that any platform guarantees the result.
[Alibaba.com's sourcing guidance](https://activity.alibaba.com/page/HowItWorks/Page.html)

## 4. Agree the order and payment milestones

The purchase documents identify the parties, 40-chair quantity, approved
references, packaging, agreed delivery term, and responsibilities. They
also describe inspection, corrective work, acceptance, and change approval.

The following amounts are hypothetical teaching figures in US dollars:

| Example item | Illustrative amount |
| --- | ---: |
| 40 chairs at $90 each | $3,600 |
| Sample and its delivery, treated separately in this example | $180 |
| Independent pre-shipment inspection | $250 |
| Budgeted transport and logistics services for the agreed scope | $1,200 |
| Subtotal of these four items | $5,230 |

The subtotal excludes duties, taxes, any required testing or conformity
work, insurance, bank or exchange charges, and other unquoted costs.
It is an incomplete budget until the buyer obtains and adds them. The
figures are not a freight rate or a landed-cost estimate for any country.

The parties agree written payment milestones for this order. The buyer
verifies payment instructions through an established contact before
sending funds and retains the confirmation. The example deliberately
uses no universal deposit percentage.

## 5. Check the production batch before releasing it

When production is ready, the inspector checks the batch against the
agreed plan. The hypothetical report identifies finish damage on several
chairs and a packing arrangement that leaves exposed corners vulnerable.
The supplier is asked to correct the affected goods and packaging.

The buyer reviews evidence of the corrections and arranges the agreed
follow-up checks before authorising shipment. Inspection has a defined
scope and cannot promise that every hidden defect has been detected.
The acceptance record identifies what was checked and what remains
outside that scope.

The final packing list is prepared from the actual packed goods. The
supplier confirms the carton count, gross weights, and dimensions rather
than repeating earlier estimates.

## 6. Compare complete transport proposals

The forwarder quotes from the actual collection address to the receiving
warehouse. The buyer checks origin handling, international movement,
destination handling, clearance services, delivery, and conditional charges.
Duties and taxes are identified separately.

Because this hypothetical order has sufficient schedule flexibility, the
buyer selects an LCL sea service after comparing current proposals.
That choice is specific to the example. An urgent project or different
cargo could lead to another result.

LCL cargo is consolidated for ocean movement and separated at destination,
as Maersk describes in its service explanation. The buyer asks the forwarder
to identify the facilities and document responsibilities for this booking.
[Maersk's LCL process](https://www.maersk.com/support/faqs/2023/08/12/how-does-maersk-lcl-work)

The buyer reviews protection for the planned journey and confirms any
chosen cover before the relevant transit begins. The forwarder supplies
the booking details and the records needed for destination clearance.

## 7. Receive, count, and close the order

The warehouse receives the packing list before arrival. Its team checks
package marks and counts, records external condition, and photographs the
goods during receiving. The chairs are checked against the purchase
order before moving into installation stock.

The buyer reconciles the supplier invoice, logistics invoices, customs
records, and payment charges against the budget. The final cost record
separates goods, development or samples, inspection, logistics, taxes, and
other services. Any reusable sample or spare is recorded consistently.

The project file retains the approved specification, sample references,
inspection results, shipment documents, and receiving report. A repeat
order can now refer to a documented product revision. A real case study
would need the underlying transaction records, permission to publish,
and verified costs and dates before making claims about an actual result.
