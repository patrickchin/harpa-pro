Quality inspection works best when the buyer has already defined what
acceptance means. Before production starts, give the supplier and inspector
the same specification, approved references, and decision rules.

For a cabinet order, “good quality” leaves the board material, edge finish,
hardware, dimensions, and packing open to interpretation. Write the checks
so another person can examine the goods and report a useful result.

## Turn the product brief into inspection requirements

List the characteristics that matter to the order. Include the correct
model, materials, dimensions, finish, components, function, quantity,
labels, and packaging. Identify which document or approved sample defines
each requirement.

Make tolerances explicit where dimensions vary. Agree the measurement
method and units. If a component must fit another product, provide the
interface drawing or a reference part where practical.

Some requirements concern safety or regulated performance. Ask a competent
specialist which tests and documents are necessary for the intended use
and destination. Keep laboratory testing and regulatory conformity work
visible in the order plan; an ordinary visual inspection has a different
scope.

For destination questions, start with the country sections of the
[main buying guide](/procurement/how-to-buy-from-china#1-check-port-clearance-and-import-requirements) and confirm
the current requirements for your exact goods with the relevant authority
and broker.

## Keep an approved sample and a controlled drawing

Label the sample with its approval date, product reference, and accepted
specification version. Keep one accessible to the inspector. If the
supplier keeps another, confirm that both references match.

Photographs help explain a finish or defect, but lighting and screens
affect appearance. Decide how colour, texture, and workmanship will be
judged where those features matter. Record accepted variations rather
than asking the inspector to infer your preferences.

Issue drawings with revision identifiers. When a change is approved,
replace the old inspection reference and ask the supplier to confirm the
version in use. A current specification should be easy to identify in
the order file.

## Choose checks that match the production risk

Different stages answer different questions. A pre-production check can
examine materials and preparation. A check during production can reveal
problems while work is continuing. A final inspection examines the goods
that are ready to ship.

Intertek describes pre-production, during-production, final random
inspection, and loading-supervision services. Match the scope to the
particular product and the decisions you need to make.
[Intertek: product quality inspections](https://www.intertek.com/appliances/product-quality-inspections/)

Consider an early check when a material substitution would be expensive
to fix later. For custom joinery, the time to clarify board type and
hardware is before the whole batch is assembled. Reserve the final
check for the finished product, its quantity, and its packing.

Confirm how much of the order must be finished and packed for the booked
inspection. Providers set readiness conditions; establish yours in
advance so an inspector does not arrive to an incomplete batch.

## Agree the sampling method and its limits

For a large order, an inspector may examine a sample of units rather
than every item. Agree the sampling plan, inspection level, defect
categories, acceptance limits, and how functional checks are handled.

ISO 2859-1 describes sampling schemes indexed by an acceptance quality
limit, or AQL, for inspection of lots. Ask the inspection provider to
specify the applicable method and edition in the inspection instructions.
[ISO: sampling procedures for inspection by attributes](https://www.iso.org/standard/2859-1)

Sampling supports an acceptance decision about a lot using the agreed
plan. The result describes the sample and checks performed; untested
items and characteristics remain outside that direct observation. A
passed report should not be presented as proof that every unit is
defect-free.

For a small batch or a critical characteristic, discuss whether checking
every unit is appropriate and technically feasible. Safety requirements
may need specialist testing or controls beyond a general inspection.
Avoid choosing an AQL simply because it appeared on another buyer's report.

## Give the inspector a complete brief

Send the purchase order, current specification, drawings, sample reference,
packing requirements, product list, factory contact, and production status.
State which tests are required and who supplies any equipment or fixtures.

The report should identify the order and inspected location, describe
readiness, list the checks, show measured results, and document defects
with photographs and quantities. Ask for clear reporting of checks that
could not be completed.

SGS lists workmanship, function, quantity, packing, labelling, and shipping
marks among its final random inspection checks. Use your actual buying
requirements to define the report you commission.
[SGS: final random inspection scope](https://www.sgs.com/en/services/final-random-inspection)

Confirm who commissions the inspector and who receives the full report.
If the supplier arranges the visit, make sure your agreed scope and
access to the findings remain explicit.

## Make a written decision when something fails

Read the findings before authorising shipment or a payment linked to
acceptance. Separate a count discrepancy, cosmetic defect, functional
failure, and missing compliance document; each needs a specific response.

Ask for a corrective-action plan describing the affected goods, rework
method, completion date, and responsibility for the cost. Decide whether
the repaired goods require another inspection or additional testing.

If you accept a deviation, describe it precisely and obtain approval
from the person responsible for the design or intended use. Keep it
separate from the unchanged requirements. A discount does not resolve a
safety issue or a product that cannot be imported or installed.

## Check packing and release the shipment deliberately

Confirm carton or crate quantities, protection for vulnerable parts,
labels, and packing-list accuracy. Discuss loading supervision when
there is a particular risk of the wrong goods being loaded or the
packing being damaged during handling.

Keep the approved report and any reinspection evidence with the invoice,
packing list, and dispatch authorisation. Send the receiving team the
references they need to check arrival. The quality record should travel
with the order through delivery and any later claim.
