title: "How to pay Chinese suppliers and reduce payment risk"

Before transferring a deposit for furniture, lighting, or building finishes,
check four things: the seller's identity, the beneficiary, the order terms,
and the conditions that release your money. A fast payment method can move
funds accurately while offering little help if the goods are wrong.

Agree the payment arrangement while you can still negotiate the purchase.
Confirm available currencies, fees, and required documents with your bank or
authorised payment provider for the countries involved.

## Verify the company and beneficiary together

Use the contracting seller's legal name when checking invoices and payment
instructions. Compare the beneficiary name with the business you verified.
Pause if the invoice introduces a different company, a personal account, or
an unexplained receiving arrangement.

A platform or authorised payment service may direct you to its own collection
account. Verify that instruction inside the provider's authenticated system
and confirm which order it credits. A salesperson's screenshot does not
establish that a receiving account belongs to the service.

Keep a record of how you verified the instructions and who approved the
payment. A small transfer can test whether money reaches an account; it does
not prove that the account holder will fulfil a large order.

## Treat bank-detail changes as a new verification task

If a supplier emails new bank details, use a previously verified contact
channel to confirm the request. Obtain the contact independently of the
message asking for the change. Check the full sender address and avoid
letting urgency replace the verification step.

The [FBI's business email compromise guidance](https://www.fbi.gov/how-we-can-help-you/common-frauds-and-scams/business-email-compromise)
recommends verifying changes to account numbers or payment procedures.
A message can appear within a familiar conversation after an account has
been compromised.

For a project team, separate preparation from approval where practical.
One person can assemble the invoice and verification evidence; another
checks the beneficiary and authorises the payment. Repeat the check when
instructions change.

## Understand what each arrangement does

| Arrangement | What to confirm before using it |
|---|---|
| Direct bank transfer | Beneficiary, fees, currency, timing, and your recovery options |
| Platform payment | Eligible order, approved payment route, coverage conditions, and dispute deadlines |
| Independent escrow | Provider status, release conditions, inspection period, fees, and dispute process |
| Letter of credit | Required documents, bank charges, discrepancies, and the conditions for payment |
| Documentary collection | Document-release instructions and the banks' limited role |

A bank transfer pays the beneficiary. It does not turn the bank into the
product inspector. Paying everything in advance commits your funds before
you see the shipment. The International Trade Administration's
[cash-in-advance guidance](https://www.trade.gov/cash-advance) explains the
buyer and seller trade-off and describes escrow as a separate arrangement
with agreed release conditions.

Use any credit-card or wallet protection only after checking eligibility
with the issuer or provider. Commercial purchases, customised goods, payment
routes, and reporting deadlines can affect the options available to you.
Do not build the purchase around an assumed chargeback or refund.

## Put specifications inside the protected order

If using Alibaba.com Trade Assurance, create the applicable online order and
follow its payment instructions. Alibaba's [buyer help guidance](https://helpcenter.alibaba.com/s/buyer/knowledge?categoryId=275875291&m_station=buyer&questionId=92572963d7b2496680fabbb2038ac60asgvpc278077001)
states that payments made outside the platform are not protected under that
service.

Read the terms shown for your transaction before paying. Record the product
specification, quantity, agreed dates, inspection arrangements, and remedies
in the order and its accepted attachments. Preserve the communication
record and note the applicable refund or dispute deadline.

A brief description such as “premium brass lights” leaves several buying
decisions unclear. Add the model, dimensions, materials, finish reference,
electrical details, packing, and required product documents. The payment
method cannot resolve ambiguity in what you agreed to purchase.

Check [Alibaba's current Trade Assurance information](https://tradeassurance.alibaba.com/)
for the services and conditions relevant to the order. Availability and
coverage should be verified for the actual transaction rather than inferred
from a supplier badge.

## Tie payment milestones to agreed evidence

A manufacturer may request a deposit to start production and a later balance.
Negotiate the actual amounts and triggers. A proposed percentage is a
commercial term for that order, not a standard you must accept.

Describe each trigger precisely. Examples to discuss include approval of
the production sample, completion of an agreed inspection, correction of
identified defects, and provision of required shipping documents. Make sure
the contract or platform order actually supports the intended release
conditions.

Decide what happens after a failed inspection. Agree who pays for rework,
reinspection, and storage, and who can approve shipment. A clause saying
“balance after production” may require payment at a different point from
“balance after the specified acceptance checks.”

Inspect against the approved sample and written specification. Keep the
inspection report attached to the purchase record. Arrange any product
compliance checks separately where the destination requires them.

## Consider bank trade instruments for larger orders

A letter of credit makes payment conditional on the specified documents.
Ask your bank and the supplier's bank about suitability, costs, and document
requirements before agreeing one. The International Trade Administration's
[letter-of-credit guide](https://www.trade.gov/letter-credit) describes the
process and the risk of document discrepancies.

Banks deal with documents under the instrument; they do not inspect the
goods themselves. The US Office of the Comptroller of the Currency explains
that limitation in its [trade finance handbook](https://www.comptrollerofthecurrency.gov/publications-and-resources/publications/comptrollers-handbook/files/trade-finance-services/pub-ch-trade-finance.pdf).
Discuss an appropriate independent inspection document with the bank if
your transaction needs one.

A documentary collection has a different function. Banks handle documents
and collection instructions, but do not provide the same payment undertaking
as a letter of credit. Review that distinction with your bank using the
[International Trade Administration's explanation](https://www.trade.gov/documentary-collections).

## Keep payment, shipment, and receipts connected

For every transfer, save the invoice, order reference, verified instructions,
approval record, and payment confirmation. Ask the supplier to confirm which
invoice the received amount settles and whether charges created a shortfall.

If you discover a suspicious payment, contact your sending bank or provider
immediately through its official channel and request its fraud-response
process. Preserve the messages and transaction details. Speed matters, and
a recall or recovery request does not guarantee that funds will return.

Before the next milestone, check the outstanding balance against the order
and the agreed evidence. Authorise each payment using the current purchase
record rather than a forwarded invoice alone.
