A supplier can send a convincing catalogue and still be the wrong business
for your order. Before paying a deposit, establish who will sell the goods,
who will make them, and what evidence supports their ability to meet your
specification.

For an interior project, that means checking more than a photograph of a
finished cabinet. You need to know which workshop produces the carcass,
who applies the finish, and whether the quoted hardware is actually included.
The same method works for lighting, tiles, machinery, and other products.

## Start with a specification suppliers can answer

Prepare a short buying brief before searching. Include the product,
quantity, material, dimensions, finish, destination, and required delivery
window. Add drawings where a description could be interpreted differently.
For lighting, include the electrical and installation requirements agreed
with the project engineer.

Separate essential requirements from preferences. A supplier should be able
to explain which requirements it can meet, which need development, and which
it cannot supply. A reply that agrees to everything without asking about
missing dimensions deserves another conversation.

Search through more than one route: wholesale marketplaces, trade fairs,
industry contacts, and manufacturers identified through relevant products.
Record the source of each lead. A recommendation is useful background;
you still need to verify the business behind it.

## Establish the contracting company's identity

Ask for the registered Chinese company name, Unified Social Credit Code,
business licence, operating address, and the name that will appear on the
contract and invoice. Keep the documents in the order file.

China's National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System provides
company information. Beijing's market regulator recommends checking
registration status, business scope, administrative information, and
abnormal-operation or serious-violation listings. Use the registered name
or credit code, with help from a Chinese-speaking colleague if needed.
[Beijing market regulator's company-check guidance](https://scjgj.beijing.gov.cn/zwxx/scjgdt/202503/t20250324_4041940.html)

Compare the result with the licence and proposed contract. Investigate
differences in company names or addresses. An export company and a factory
may legitimately be separate entities, but their relationship and
responsibilities should be documented before payment.

Business registration establishes part of the company's identity. It does
not establish that the company can manufacture your product or fulfil your
order. Keep those checks separate.

## Find out where your goods will be made

Ask whether the seller manufactures the quoted goods, buys them from another
factory, or combines both roles. A trading company may be suitable if it
coordinates several categories well. Your concern is clarity about who
controls production and resolves problems.

Request evidence relevant to the actual product:

- Recent production photographs or a live walkthrough of the relevant line.
- A description of materials, machines, outsourced processes, and capacity.
- Examples of comparable work, with customer details shared only with consent.
- A proposed production schedule showing approval and inspection points.

A live video can help you understand a workshop. For a substantial or
technically demanding order, commission an independent factory assessment
with a written scope. Give the assessor your drawings and questions so the
visit evaluates the work you need.

On Alibaba.com, open the assessment report behind a Verified Supplier badge.
The platform describes third-party on-site assessment and reports available
to buyers. Check the report date, assessed legal entity, business type, and
relevant production processes. A badge alone is insufficient order evidence.
[Alibaba.com's Verified Supplier programme](https://seller.alibaba.com/verified-supplier?locale=en)

## Check documents against the exact product

If the supplier provides a test report, compare its model, material,
manufacturer, test standard, issuing body, and date with the goods quoted.
A report about one board material does not establish the performance of a
finished cabinet containing different boards and adhesives.

Confirm authenticity with the issuing laboratory or certification body
through its independently located contact details. Ask your destination
broker or relevant authority which evidence is required for import and use.
The existing [buying guide's country sections](/procurement/how-to-buy-from-china#1-check-port-clearance-and-import-requirements)
are a starting point for destination questions; requirements depend on the
actual goods and market.

Keep an evidence table with a row for each essential claim. Record the
document received, what it covers, who checked it, and anything still
unresolved. This makes gaps visible before a deadline encourages a rushed
decision.

## Use a sample to test the working relationship

Order a sample made to your written specification. Confirm whether it is
stock, modified stock, or a newly made sample. Check how the supplier handles
questions, changes, packaging, and dispatch as well as the product itself.

Measure the sample and record the result. For a cabinet handle, check fixing
centres, finish, edges, and supplied screws. For a chair, agree the checks
with a competent product specialist. Some safety and performance requirements
need laboratory testing rather than a visual review.

Approve the sample in writing, label it, and keep a reference accessible to
the production inspector. Sample approval defines a reference for the
order. Batch inspection is a later, separate decision.

## Resolve the commercial details before the deposit

Put the agreed specification, approved sample reference, quantity, price,
packaging, production timing, inspection access, and remedy for defects into
the order documents. Name the company responsible for delivering that order.

Verify payment instructions through a known contact using a separate channel
before transferring money. Explain and document any difference between the
contracting business and the beneficiary. If you are relying on platform
protection, check the order's eligibility and payment instructions inside
the platform.

Compare suppliers using the same brief. Mark each requirement as confirmed,
unconfirmed, or unacceptable. Keep unresolved safety, identity, and payment
questions open until evidence answers them. A lower price cannot close those
questions.

## Keep a decision record

Before approving the supplier, record who checked the legal entity, who
approved the sample, and who confirmed the import requirements. Save the
chosen quotation and the reasons for accepting it.

That record also defines what needs checking again on a repeat order:
materials, factory location, production scope, payment details, and the
current specification. Verification should follow the business and order
you are actually buying from today.
