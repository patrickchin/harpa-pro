A furniture quote marked EXW and another marked DDP cover different parts
of the journey. Compare them only after identifying the services and costs
between the factory and your project address.

Incoterms® rules allocate delivery responsibilities, certain costs, and the
risk of loss or damage between buyer and seller. They do not specify every
purchase condition. Agree payment, ownership, product quality, remedies,
and dispute arrangements in the sales contract. ICC Academy explains these
boundaries in its [guide to Incoterms and the contract of sale](https://academy.iccwbo.org/incoterms/article/incoterms-and-contract-of-sale/).

## Write the rule and exact place together

A useful reference includes the rule, a named place or port, and the version.
For example: “FCA [supplier's full factory address], Incoterms® 2020.” Replace
the bracketed address with the actual agreed location and delivery point.

Avoid a bare acronym on a quotation. The named location helps identify
where an obligation ends. Under some rules it marks the destination the
seller pays to reach, while risk transfers earlier in the journey.

This table summarises the normal delivery points. The full rules and your
contract address notice obligations and circumstances that a short table
cannot capture.

| Rule | Transport modes | Normal delivery and risk-transfer point |
|---|---|---|
| EXW, Ex Works | Any mode | Goods made available at the named place, ready for collection and not loaded |
| FCA, Free Carrier | Any mode | Delivery to the buyer's nominated carrier or person at the agreed point |
| FOB, Free On Board | Sea or inland waterway | Goods placed on board the vessel at the shipment port |
| CIF, Cost, Insurance and Freight | Sea or inland waterway | Goods placed on board at the shipment port, although the seller pays freight and insurance to the destination port |
| DDP, Delivered Duty Paid | Any mode | Import-cleared goods available on the arriving vehicle at the named destination, ready for unloading |

ICC's [Incoterms 2020 questions and answers](https://library.iccwbo.org/clp/clp-incoterms-qa-2020.htm?AGENT=ICC_HQ)
sets out the division between rules for any transport mode and rules for sea
or inland waterway transport.

## EXW leaves the collection arrangements with the buyer

Under EXW, the seller makes the goods available at the named place. The
buyer arranges loading, transport, and export and import clearance, where
applicable. Establish who will perform those tasks before accepting the
quote.

For an overseas buyer, export formalities can make EXW difficult to organise.
Ask the forwarder which entity will handle export clearance and what
assistance is required from the seller. ICC's [selection checklist](https://library.iccwbo.org/content/clp/Others/incoterms_2020_checklist_2024-update.pdf)
flags EXW as primarily suited to domestic trade and identifies possible
export difficulties.

If you need the seller to load the collecting vehicle and handle export
clearance, discuss FCA at the supplier's premises and price that scope.

## FCA can match a container or multimodal handover

Under FCA, the seller handles export clearance. At the seller's premises,
delivery occurs when the goods are loaded onto the buyer's collecting
vehicle. At another agreed location, the seller delivers them ready for
unloading from its arriving vehicle to the buyer's nominated carrier or
person.

FCA works across transport modes. It can fit furniture collected by truck
and later loaded into a sea container. Confirm the precise terminal or
warehouse and check the loading or unloading arrangements in the quote.
See [ICC Academy's FCA and FOB comparison](https://academy.iccwbo.org/incoterms/article/incoterms-2020-fca-or-fob/).

## FOB uses an on-board delivery point

FOB applies to sea or inland waterway transport. The seller clears the
goods for export and delivers them on board the vessel at the named
shipment port. The buyer arranges the main carriage and normally bears
transport risk after that delivery point.

Containerised goods are often handed to a carrier at a terminal before
vessel loading. Discuss FCA when that earlier handover matches the actual
arrangement. Ask the forwarder to explain how the proposed delivery point
will work for your shipment. ICC's [selection checklist](https://library.iccwbo.org/content/clp/Others/incoterms_2020_checklist_2024-update.pdf)
distinguishes container and terminal delivery from direct on-board delivery.

## CIF separates the freight bill from the risk point

Under CIF, the seller arranges and pays for carriage and the required
insurance to the named destination port. The normal risk-transfer point
remains on board the vessel at the shipment port.

That distinction matters if goods are damaged at sea. Ask for the insurance
document and claims instructions. CIF's default insurance follows Institute Cargo
Clauses (C) or similar cover, with an insured amount of at least 110% of
the contract price. Check the exclusions and agree additional protection
if the goods need it. CIF applies to sea and inland waterway transport.
See [ICC Academy's CIF and CIP explanation](https://academy.iccwbo.org/incoterms/article/incoterms-2020-cip-or-cif/).

The seller pays destination unloading if its carriage contract includes
that charge; otherwise it is for the buyer. Confirm this before adding
unloading to your own budget. [ICC: CIF cost and insurance responsibilities](https://library.iccwbo.org/content/tfb/BOOKS/BK_0049/BK_0049_05_RulesSea.htm?AGENT=ICC_HQ)

Confirm destination handling and onward-delivery charges. A named destination
port does not, by itself, mean delivery into the client's building.

## DDP needs a workable import arrangement

Under DDP, the seller arranges delivery to the named destination and handles
export and import clearance, including applicable import duties and taxes.
Delivery occurs with the goods on the arriving vehicle, ready for unloading.
Agree unloading and any associated costs explicitly. These responsibilities
are explained in [ICC Academy's EXW and DDP comparison](https://academy.iccwbo.org/incoterms/article/incoterms-2020-exw-or-ddp/).

Confirm how the seller will meet destination requirements. Some countries
require a local entity for import clearance, which can complicate DDP.
ICC discusses this in its [report on national regulatory barriers](https://academy.iccwbo.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/2025-icc-national-regulatory-barriers-to-the-incoterms-2020-rules-v2.pdf).

Ask who will be named on the declaration, which documents you receive,
and how duties and taxes are recorded. Never accept false values or product
descriptions as a way to make a delivery quote work.

## Compare a real shipment using the same endpoint

For an illustrative furniture order, put the supplier quote beside a cost
list running to the actual project address. Add origin collection, export
services, main carriage, insurance, destination charges, import charges,
and final delivery only where they are outside the quoted scope.

Then make a separate line showing where risk transfers. Under CIF, for
example, a seller-paid freight bill extends further than the seller's
normal transport risk. Check insurance against that exposure.

Before signing, confirm the rule with your supplier, forwarder, and clearing
agent. Record the exact place, services, documents, and charges in the
purchase agreement. That gives you a usable comparison and a shared set of
instructions for the shipment.
