When a shipment arrives with broken fittings or missing cartons, the first
job is to establish what happened. Keep the receiving records clear enough
for the supplier, forwarder, carrier, insurer, or platform to investigate.
Each may need different evidence and follow a different process.

Act promptly. The relevant notice periods and filing requirements depend
on the contract, transport service, policy, platform order, and applicable
law. Check them immediately rather than waiting for a supplier to finish
an informal investigation.

## Record the delivery before moving everything into use

Give the receiving team the packing list before arrival. At delivery,
compare the package count and marks with the transport records. Photograph
the cargo's condition, labels, and any visible damage before unpacking.

Where possible, record specific exceptions on the delivery receipt and
retain a copy. “Two cartons missing” or “crate corner crushed” gives more
information than a general complaint. Ask the delivering party how to
record exceptions through their system.

Keep original packaging and affected contents available for inspection.
FedEx's claims guidance expressly asks customers to retain original
packaging and identifies documents such as invoices and repair evidence
that may support a claim. Follow the process for the service actually used.
[FedEx's claims guidance](https://www.fedex.com/en-us/customer-support/claims.html)

## Separate the problems in an itemised report

Use one line per affected product or package. Record the ordered quantity,
received quantity, affected quantity, purchase-order reference, carton
mark, and a factual description.

| Observation | Evidence to collect | Parties to notify or investigate with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crushed crate and broken contents | Delivery notes, external and internal photographs, packing records | Forwarder or carrier, insurer, and supplier |
| Correct carton count but too few items inside | Packing list, carton marks, counts, weights, and opening records | Supplier and any relevant warehouse or logistics operator |
| Goods differ from the approved specification | Signed specification, sample reference, measurements, batch details | Supplier and eligible purchasing platform |
| Entire shipment remains undelivered | Booking, tracking, transport documents, delivery instructions | Contracting logistics party, supplier, and insurer as relevant |

These are investigation starting points. A broken item can result from
poor packing, transport damage, or a manufacturing weakness. Avoid
assigning responsibility before reviewing the evidence and agreements.

## Preserve usable evidence

Photograph the complete package and its label before close-ups. Show a
measurement or comparison where it demonstrates the discrepancy. Keep
original files, record when the goods were examined, and identify who
conducted the check.

For a wrong finish, photograph the received item beside the approved
physical sample under consistent lighting. For a dimensional error, record
the drawing reference and measuring method. An independent inspection
may be useful where the value or technical disagreement justifies it.

Store the order, specifications, approved changes, invoice, payment record,
packing list, inspection report, shipping documents, and delivery receipt
together. An opening video can help explain the sequence, but it should
support a complete evidence file.

## Notify the relevant parties separately

Contact the seller about goods that fail to match the order. Notify the
contracting logistics party about delivery damage, loss, or handling
concerns. Where cargo insurance is in place, notify the insurer or claims
contact according to the policy.

Carrier liability and cargo insurance are different arrangements. The
carrier's obligations and limits may leave a gap between the cargo value
and a recoverable claim. Review the applicable transport contract and
insurance wording rather than treating the freight payment as full-value
protection.
[DHL's cargo-protection explanation](https://www.dhl.com/us-en/home/ship/shipping-faq.html)

Ask each party to acknowledge your notification and provide a reference.
Request the next required action, evidence, and deadline in writing.
Track these separately. Informal supplier discussions may continue while
another claim process requires action.

## Use the platform process where it applies

If the order was purchased through an eligible protection programme,
check its terms and the options shown on the actual order. Record the
problem in the platform's dispute or refund workflow and upload the
requested evidence within its deadlines.

Alibaba.com's current refund guidance includes missing orders and product
issues, with supplier negotiation and escalation options. Eligibility,
timing, and the outcome depend on the order and applicable terms.
[Alibaba.com's refund process](https://tradeassurance.alibaba.com/ta/MoneyBackPolicy.htm)

Keep settlement discussions and agreed product requirements in the
relevant order records. A promise in a separate message may leave the
formal case unresolved. Check what happens to a dispute before cancelling
it, confirming acceptance, or agreeing to a later remedy.

## Ask for a remedy that solves the actual problem

State what you want and why it is workable. Options may include replacement
parts, repair, replacement goods, a partial refund, or return and refund.
The remedy available depends on the agreement and circumstances.

For a project, price the full proposed fix. Replacement cabinet doors
may require freight, import handling, and installation. A credit against
a future order may have little value if you will never buy again.

Record quantities, specifications, payment or credit arrangements,
delivery responsibilities, and the agreed completion conditions. Confirm
who pays transport, duties, inspection, and repair labour. Keep a record
of recovered amounts so the same loss is represented accurately across
claims.

## Preserve the goods while limiting further loss

Move affected goods to suitable storage and separate them from stock
approved for use. For goods with a suspected safety fault, stop their
use or distribution and obtain appropriate technical advice.

Ask the claims handler before repair, return, disposal, or alteration that
could prevent inspection. Where urgent protective work is necessary,
document the condition first and retain receipts. Request written
instructions for any return and check its transport and customs
arrangements before dispatch.

Once resolved, add the cause and corrective action to your next purchase
specification. Change the packaging, inspection, count checks, or component
requirements that would have helped identify or prevent this particular
failure.
