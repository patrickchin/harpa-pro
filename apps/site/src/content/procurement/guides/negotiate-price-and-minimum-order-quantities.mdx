Your interiors project needs 60 chairs. The supplier quotes a minimum of 200,
and its lowest advertised unit price applies to 500. Before asking for a
discount, find out what drives those quantities and which parts of your
requirement can change.

A useful negotiation produces an order your project can afford, receive,
and use. Keep the approved design and safety requirements visible while
discussing the price.

## Ask what the minimum quantity applies to

Minimum order quantity, or MOQ, is the smallest order a supplier agrees to
accept. Confirm it for the exact product and finish. Ask whether the minimum
applies per model, colour, material, size, or total order.

A chair frame might have one minimum while its custom upholstery fabric has
another. Ask the supplier to explain the actual constraint. A written answer
lets you test a specific alternative instead of repeating “Can you reduce
the MOQ?”

Alibaba's [buyer guidance on MOQ](https://seller.alibaba.com/businessblogs/px6g2lut-what-is-minimum-order-quantity-moq)
notes that some suppliers will negotiate their minimums. Treat flexibility
as something to confirm for your order, rather than a promise attached to
every listing.

For fitted joinery, check how the supplier counts units. A project comprising
several differently sized cabinets may need a schedule of openings and
components before the manufacturer can offer a meaningful minimum.

## Separate the parts you can change from those you must keep

Mark the requirements that the completed project depends on. These might
include dimensions, structural performance, electrical compatibility, finish
matching, and required compliance documents. Obtain the client's approval
before changing a visible or functional design requirement.

Then list possible variables. Could you use a stocked fabric, reduce the
number of finish variants, use standard cartons, or accept a later dispatch
window? Ask the supplier to price each change separately.

For example, request one quote for the specified custom fabric and another
for an approved stock fabric. Keep the material references on both quotes.
A lower price becomes useful only when you know which product it buys.

Preserve protective packing when negotiating decorative goods. Ask for a
packing drawing or sample where the product needs it. Replacing a protective
insert with a thinner alternative should be a documented decision with an
agreed way to assess transport suitability.

## Offer a smaller order with an explicit trade-off

A supplier might agree to a trial quantity at a higher unit price or with a
separate setup charge. Ask for the total rather than refusing an increase
on principle. Paying more per item can require less cash than purchasing
unneeded stock.

The following figures are hypothetical and show only the arithmetic:

| Offer | Quantity | Unit price | Setup charge | Goods and setup total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Larger production run | 200 | USD 45 | USD 0 | USD 9,000 |
| Trial run | 60 | USD 55 | USD 150 | USD 3,450 |

The trial run has a higher cost per chair and a lower initial goods bill.
Compare freight, inspections, import charges, and replacement needs before
choosing. These figures are not indicative supplier prices or a prediction
of what a factory will accept.

Also calculate where surplus stock would go. Storage, handling, cash tied
up, and the risk of later design changes belong in the decision. Use the
quantity you expect to need, supported by the project schedule.

## Ask for volume tiers you can genuinely consider

Request prices at your required quantity and at one or two realistic higher
quantities. Keep the specification and delivery basis the same across the
tiers. Ask whether one-time costs change and whether the lead time remains
valid.

Tell the supplier when future orders are tentative. A possible pipeline is
useful context, but it should not become a purchase promise. If the supplier
offers a sample credit or repeat-order rebate, agree the conditions and
expiry in writing.

Avoid committing to later quantities just to obtain today's price. Check
whether the proposed arrangement includes a binding minimum, storage
charge, cancellation condition, or obligation to buy unused materials.
Seek advice on an agreement that would create a substantial future
commitment.

## Negotiate the whole order alongside price

A price reduction can be offset by less helpful payment or delivery terms.
Compare each revised offer against your original requirements. Record
changes to deposit timing, inspection access, production time, packing,
document support, and defect handling.

Alibaba's [questions to ask before a B2B order](https://activity.alibaba.com/page/resources/5_questions_to_ask_a_seller_before_placing_a_B2B_order.html)
include samples, minimum quantities, and shipping terms. Those questions
belong together in a negotiation because they affect what you pay and
receive.

If a supplier wants an earlier balance payment in exchange for a lower
price, consider how much money would be committed before independent checks.
If it proposes a different delivery rule, obtain the additional transport
and clearance costs needed for a fair comparison.

## Send a specific counteroffer

Use a message that gives the supplier a decision it can price. For example:

> We need 60 chairs for this project, in the single approved finish shown
> in specification CH-01 revision 3. Please quote that quantity using the
> approved stock fabric and standard protective cartons. List any setup
> charge separately. Please also quote 100 chairs on the same delivery
> basis, with the same inspection access and payment milestones. We can
> consider dispatch in either of the two weeks shown on the schedule.

This is an illustrative message. Replace the options with ones your client
and project team can accept. Ask for a revised written offer rather than
assuming that a brief chat reply updates every part of the quotation.

## Close the negotiation with a checked order

Confirm the final material references, quantities, unit prices, one-time
charges, packing, and delivery point. Write down which documents and checks
must be complete before each payment and shipment approval.

Keep the approved sample and specification tied to the final agreement.
If a concession changes the sample, arrange approval of the revised version
before production proceeds.

A supplier may still decline the smaller quantity. Record the reason and
compare another supplier or an approved stock product. Make the purchase
when the complete order meets your project requirements and budget.
